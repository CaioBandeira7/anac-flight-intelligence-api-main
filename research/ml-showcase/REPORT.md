# How Predictable Is Brazilian Aviation?

### Machine Learning for Airport Traffic, Seat Occupancy, and Route Demand

**Author:** Caio Bandeira · **Project:** ANAC Flight Intelligence v2 · **Evidence:** 2023 validation, out-of-sample

> **Scope of every number in this report.** All results are **validation results on 2023**, data the models did not see during training or early stopping. They are **not** results of the final test (2024–2025), which has **not been run** (Section 6.4), and they are **not** live forecasts: no model serves predictions in production. Every figure and table is regenerated by `research/ml-showcase/reproduce.py` and `analysis.py` from the saved artifacts.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Three forecasting problems were built on 26 years of ANAC flight-leg records, each compared against simple baselines on 2023 data that played no part in fitting:

| Model | What it forecasts | Best simple baseline | LightGBM | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Daily takeoffs + landings at the 50 busiest airports, 1 and 3 months ahead | Recent same weekday: WAPE **10.30%** | WAPE **10.10%** | **No conclusive gain.** Difference −0.21 pts, 95% interval −0.84 to +0.35. The baseline is the method of record. |
| **B** | Paid load factor per route and carrier, 1, 3 and 6 months ahead | Mean of last 12 known months: **6.54 pts** | **6.06 pts** seat-weighted MAE | **Beats the baseline**, −0.48 pts (−0.94 to −0.04). Conclusive overall and at 6 months; not on its own at 1 or 3 months. |
| **D** | Monthly paid passengers and seats per route, 1, 3 and 6 months ahead | Same month last year: WAPE **25.31%** (pax), **25.73%** (seats) | WAPE **22.24%** (pax), **22.40%** (seats) | **Beats the baseline** overall, −3.07 and −3.33 pts. **But** it under-forecasts by about **12%** and is **no better than the baseline at 6 months**. |

**What this means.**
- Airport traffic is highly predictable from its own recent rhythm (about 10% WAPE), and a gradient-boosted model adds nothing measurable on top of a simple weekday rule.
- Seat occupancy is predictable to about 6 points, and the model's gain is real but small.
- Route demand is where machine learning adds the most at short horizons: at 1 month ahead its WAPE is 18.7% against 24.1% for the best baseline.
- Route demand is also where the model fails most clearly. It inherits the level of the year before and missed 2023's 15% market growth, so it should not be used without a level correction.

**What was not done, on purpose.**
- No hyperparameter search.
- No test-set evaluation.
- No SHAP: feature attributions are LightGBM gain importances, labelled as such.
- No user-input "prediction playground" (Section 14.3 explains why).

## 2. Research Questions

1. **RQ1 — Predictability.** How accurately can airport movements, seat occupancy and route demand be forecast 1 to 6 months ahead from public ANAC data alone?
2. **RQ2 — Value of ML.** Does a gradient-boosted model beat simple, transparent baselines by more than chance?
3. **RQ3 — Where it fails.** Which airports, routes, carriers, volumes, horizons and months concentrate the errors, and is the error biased?
4. **RQ4 — Use.** Given the measured error, what can these forecasts responsibly support?

## 3. Dataset & Data Quality

**Source.** ANAC's public statistical air transport microdata, one row per flight leg, from 2000-01 to 2025-12. That is a ~20 GB CSV, ingested by an audited, resumable pipeline into the `flights` Parquet dataset. The raw file is never modified.

**Quality controls that shape the results:**

| Issue | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Four months have no data:** 2015-08, 2016-02, 2018-07, 2020-08 | Treated as **unknown, never zero.** Lags falling on them are null, and means divide by known months only. |
| **Passenger scope** | Flights that took off with seats, excluding positioning, zero-distance and same-airport legs. |
| **Load factor above 1.5** | Treated as a recording error for B and D (12,710 legs across all years). Values from 1 to 1.5 are kept. |
| **Per-leg counting** | A passenger on a two-leg flight counts twice. Totals run about 1–2% above ANAC's published figures. |
| **No fares** | The data has no ticket prices, so no price-related target exists. |

**Model datasets** (target months; the test period is not written to disk):

| Module | Unit | Train | Purged | Validation | Inputs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Airport × day, top 50 airports | 356,050 rows (2013-01 → 2022-10) | 9,150 | 36,500 | 21 (10 calendar, 6 history, airport, timing) |
| B | Route × carrier × month | 488,226 (2013-01 → 2022-10) | 15,591 | 47,197, of which 42,369 flew and were scored | 42 (29 history, 6 calendar, origin, destination, carrier, distance, …) |
| D | Route × month | 318,149 (2013-01 → 2022-10) | 11,052 | 33,762 | 38 (26 history, 6 calendar, origin, destination, distance, …) |

In B, about 90% of eligible units flew in the target month. Its errors are **conditional on the unit flying**: the model does not predict whether a route is cancelled.

## 5. Feature Engineering

Every feature is **point-in-time**:

1. At the start of origin month *O*, the last month known is *C = O − 2*, because publication lags by about two months.
2. The target is *T = O + h*.
3. Feature functions receive history already cut at *C* and raise `LeakageError` if a later row reaches them.
4. Targets are built in separate functions and stored in separate files.
5. Tests scramble or delete everything after the cutoff and require identical features (`tests/test_features_leakage.py`, `tests/test_real_features.py`).

| Group | Examples | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar | day of week, week of year, month, school vacation, holidays, Carnival, days in month, weekend days | A, B, D |
| History (level) | recent 28/56-day level, same weekday mean over 8 weeks, means over 3/6/12 months | A, B, D |
| History (seasonal) | lag 364/728 days; lag 12/24 months; year-over-year change | A, B, D |
| Load factor history | route, carrier, origin and destination load factor; volatility | B, D |
| Structure | seats per flight, carriers on route, months observed, unknown months in window | B, D |
| Identity | airport; origin, destination, carrier; distance; international flag | A, B, D |

Absolute time (the year) is excluded on purpose, so trees cannot memorise a calendar period.

**Scale-free targets.** Volumes (A, D) are modelled as a ratio to the unit's recent level, because trees cannot extrapolate. With L1 loss weighted by that level, |y − p·level| = level·|y/level − p|. The model therefore minimises WAPE directly.

## 6. Experimental Design

### 6.1 Splits

| Split | Target months | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Train | 2013-01 → 2022-10 (h = 1); earlier for longer horizons | Fitting |
| Early stopping | Targets from 2022-01 within training | Choosing the number of trees |
| Purge | Targets not yet known at the first validation origin | Discarded, to prevent look-ahead |
| **Validation** | **2023-01 → 2023-12** | **All results in this report** |
| Test (sealed) | 2024-01 → 2025-12 | Not built, not read, not run |

### 6.2 Protocol fixed in advance

- **One configuration.** A single conservative LightGBM configuration (`configs/models.toml`), fixed before any validation result was seen. No hyperparameter search.
  - Settings: `objective = l1`, `learning_rate = 0.03`, `num_leaves = 31`, `min_data_in_leaf = 200`, `feature_fraction` and `bagging_fraction` 0.8, `lambda_l2 = 5`, `seed = 42`, `deterministic = true`.
- **Selection rule, fixed in advance.** A model replaces its best baseline only if the 95% interval of the paired error difference excludes zero.
- **Uncertainty.** Intervals come from a **block bootstrap over the 12 validation months**, so they respect within-month correlation.
- **Freezing.** After validation, each model was refit on everything known at the first test origin, with the same number of rounds, and frozen in `models/v1` with SHA-256 hashes. `test_period_used: false` is recorded in each manifest.

### 6.3 Reproduction (this study)

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Metrics recomputed from the saved validation predictions with the project's own `compare()` | Identical to `reports/models/*.json` (difference < 1e-12) for A, B, D-pax and D-seats |
| Selection models **refit from scratch in memory** with the frozen configuration | **Bit-identical predictions** (maximum absolute difference 0.0) and the same best iteration (98, 46, 395, 243) |
| SHA-256 of `models/v1/*/*.txt` against the manifests | All four match |
| Environment | Python 3.12.10, LightGBM 4.7.0, Polars 2.1.0, NumPy 2.5.4 |

Full record: `research/ml-showcase/results/reproduction.json`.

### 6.4 Why the final test was not run

The brief allowed one final evaluation on 2024–2025 only with evidence that the period stayed untouched. That evidence is incomplete:

1. **Decision 3** in `docs/decisions.md` records that annual totals for 2024–2025 were seen during the data audit. The test period is therefore **not fully blind**.
2. The project's own plan (`docs/models.md`, known issue 6) is to retrain on a dataset with the four missing months recovered, freeze as `models/v2`, and then test. Testing `v1` now would spend the test period on models already scheduled for replacement.
3. The 2024–2025 market totals are visible in the dashboard and in Figure 1. Any further look at that period, including this study, makes it less blind.

The test was therefore **not executed**. The frozen models and their hashes are ready for the test once the owner unseals it under the documented procedure.

## 8. Evaluation Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **WAPE** | Σ\|ŷ − y\| / Σ\|y\| | Scale-free for volumes, robust to small units, interpretable as "share of traffic misplaced" |
| **Seat-weighted MAE** | Σ seats·\|ŷ − y\| / Σ seats, in percentage points | Load factor is already a ratio; weighting by seats reflects capacity |
| **Bias** | Σ(ŷ − y) / Σ\|y\| | Systematic over (+) or under (−) forecasting |
| **RMSE** | √mean((ŷ − y)²) | Penalises large misses (A, D only) |
| **95% interval** | Block bootstrap over 12 months | Uncertainty of each metric and of paired differences |

## 13. Explainability

The only attribution available is **LightGBM split gain** from the selection models (`reports/models/*.json`). It shows what the trees split on. It is **not** a causal effect and **not** SHAP. No SHAP analysis was run.

![A gain](figures/a_feature_gain.png)
![B gain](figures/b_feature_gain.png)
![D gain](figures/d_pax_feature_gain.png)

| Model | Top features by gain |
|---|---|
| A | Day of week 30.8%, airport 17.8%, week of year 9.2%, weekend 6.9%, month 6.7% |
| B | Load factor 12 months earlier 51.5%, 24 months earlier 10.0%, 12-month mean 9.4%, 6-month mean 7.3% |

## 14. Practical Applications

### 14.1 What the evidence supports

| Use | Model | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Airport staffing and slot planning, 1–3 months ahead, at major airports | **Baseline A** (recent same weekday) | About 10% WAPE. At large airports such as GRU, about 4–6%. |
| Load-factor expectation per route and carrier, for capacity reviews | **LightGBM B** | About 6 pts MAE, unbiased. Strongest at 6 months and on domestic routes. |
| Relative route demand: ranking and seasonality, 1–3 months ahead | **LightGBM D** | Shape and ranking are better than last year's values. Levels need a market-level correction. |

### 14.2 What it does not support

- **Absolute route demand without a level correction:** Model D runs about 12% low in a growth year.
- **Six months ahead for Model D:** no better than last year.
- **Structural changes,** such as an airport losing traffic: no history-based model sees them coming.
- **Unconstrained demand or price:** the data measures passengers carried, not demand, and has no fares.

### 14.3 Why there is no free-input prediction playground

The models consume dozens of features computed from history at a cutoff: lags, rolling means, year-over-year change and calendar effects. A user cannot type these honestly. A form would either ask for values only known after the target month, which is leakage, or silently invent them.

The frozen `v1` models have also not been tested beyond 2023, Module A's model does not beat its baseline, and no serving path builds features for new origins.

The platform therefore shows an **interactive explorer of the verified 2023 out-of-sample predictions**, and no form that generates new numbers.

## 15. Limitations & Threats to Validity

1. **One validation year, and an atypical one.** 2023 was a recovery year with +15.3% growth, and the results may not transfer to a flat year. The block bootstrap covers sampling noise within 2023, not year-to-year change.
2. **Test not run** (Section 6.4). The test period is also not fully blind (decision 3).
3. **Four absent months** in the history weaken the lags that fall on them.
4. **Revisions.** Backtests use revised data, since no historical vintages exist (decision 5). This may flatter the most recent months.
5. **Conditional evaluation in B:** routes that stopped flying are not scored.
6. **Per-leg counting** and a snapshot of the source that predates later ANAC revisions.
7. **Gain importance is not attribution.** No causal claims are made.
8. **Baselines were reported before the models** (`reports/baselines/`, `docs/features.md`). The model configuration was fixed before validation, but the baselines were known, as is standard.

## 17. Conclusions & Future Work

**Answers to the research questions:**

- **RQ1:**
  - Airport movements: about 10% WAPE.
  - Seat occupancy: about 6 pts MAE.
  - Route demand: about 19% WAPE at 1 month, rising to about 27% at 6.
- **RQ2:** Machine learning beats simple baselines for **B** (small, conclusive) and **D** (clear at 1–3 months). It does not for **A**, where a weekday rule is as good.
- **RQ3:** Errors concentrate in small units, in structural breaks (SBRJ in late 2023), in international routes for B, and in a level bias for D during growth.
- **RQ4:** The models are useful for planning signals and relative comparisons, with the stated error bands. They do not replace absolute forecasts in a regime change.

**Next steps, in order:**

1. Recover the four absent months and retrain as `models/v2`.
2. Run the sealed 2024–2025 test once, without tuning.
3. Add a market-level correction, or the year-over-year level of the origin month, to D, and evaluate it on validation before the test.
4. Report results per year when more validation years become available.
5. Serve forecasts only after the test, with the error bands above attached.
